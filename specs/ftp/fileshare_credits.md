# DarkSpeak FileShare Credits — optional exchange

**Status:** Independent optional FTP sub-feature; **deferred** until read-only browsing/downloads are interactively verified and uploads work. **Default OFF for every local identity**, including new identities and upgraded identities without an explicit saved choice.

**Related:** `fileshare_authorization.md` (ordinary access permissions and hard resource limits), `fileshare_uploads.md` (upload acceptance/publication), `../protocol.md` (generic mutually verified checkpoints), `../transfer_queue.md` (work scheduling). The generic transfer engine, ordinary messaging, directory listings, previews, and basic FTP must function without the ledger.

## 1. Identity feature switch and disabled behavior

Persist `credits_enabled: bool` **per local identity**, default false. Only a local authorized operator may change it; contact/group policies cannot enable exchange on an otherwise disabled identity. The toggle affects the entire identity's FileShare service, not one contact or mount.

**When disabled:** FileShare downloads are governed solely by ordinary READ/LIST/VFS permissions, content integrity, quotas and resource limits; no credit reservations, debits, awards or credit-related waiting/pause reasons are applied. Incoming uploads remain subject to normal file acceptance rules but earn no credits. Do not initialize a mandatory accounting dependency or reject basic FTP merely because credit ledger tables are unavailable. Other feature transfers and catalog/previews remain exempt regardless of switch setting.

**Enabling:** perform a versioned, recoverable feature-state transition. Ensure ledger/coordinator availability before allowing *new* credit-gated admissions; if startup/recovery fails, fail closed for new chargeable transfers while preserving basic read-only FTP only in a clearly operator-selected disabled mode—not by silently falling back and bypassing enabled accounting. Credit configuration defaults to **unrestricted/free downloads until the operator explicitly configures a credit requirement**; enabling does not retroactively charge completed transfers or automatically award old accepted uploads.

**Disabling:** atomically mark the identity disabled for new credit operations and stop further credit settlement at a documented verified checkpoint boundary for work admitted under the previous enabled epoch. Preserve already settled balances, entitlements, charge identities, and audit/settlement evidence; release outstanding unspent reservations after reconciliation. Resume remaining authorized content as *uncharged* work only after the epoch transition and outstanding checkpoint/charge uncertainty have been resolved, using a fresh trusted local admission decision. Never retroactively refund old legitimate debits or invent new awards, and never delete ledger history merely because the feature is off. If the transition cannot safely resolve outstanding work, complete the switch only once chargeable attempts have safely paused/settled; show transitional status to operator. Re-enabling uses the same durable account history and a new policy epoch; do not automatically recharge the old transferred prefix.

**Accounting policy precedence** within an enabled identity uses contact → groups → identity defaults with most specific rule per tier and deterministic merge rules in §2. Hard physical/system budgets are never modified by this feature. Operators see the switch in Identity → FileShare settings, and its state/transition status in the credit administration UI. Credit-related controls are hidden or explicitly disabled when off, without preventing ordinary upload acceptance or download management.

## 2. Effective credit policy and group merges

All exchange-specific fields are independently optional (`UNSPECIFIED` inherits), evaluated contact → groups → identity as in `fileshare_authorization.md`. Group merge rules from the original specification are retained:

| Credit policy field | Merge among groups |
| --- | --- |
| Downloadable bytes earned per accepted uploaded byte | Most favorable ratio |
| Free/initial credit, earned-credit cap, voluntary download allowance | Highest benefit/allowance |
| Credit approval: automatic, manual, disabled | `Disabled` > `Manual` > `Automatic` |
| Optional audit/history policy | Most privacy-preserving |

A missing value inherits; explicit `Unlimited`/`Disabled` remain distinct. Ratio and downloadable credit policy are independent; all credit is **byte-based**. Ratios use bounded checked rational integers with preserved fractional remainders, not floating-point. Identity-level `credits_enabled=false` supersedes all these fields. Altering policy affects future admissions; previously captured awards/charges retain their historical policy evidence.

## 3. Credit ledger, entitlements and transfer settlement

### 3.1 Accounts, awards and charges

Maintain **separate per-local-identity, per-contact** byte-credit accounts, with an append-only, durable, transactionally applied accounting ledger and bounded checkpoints/compaction. Ledger records carry unique operation IDs, account, byte amount (checked signed representation), type (`UploadAward`, `UploadRevocation`, `DownloadDebit`, `ManualGrant`, `ManualDeduction`, `BalanceAdjustment`), coarse timestamp and optional operator reason. Upload-related records reference immutable accepted `UploadId`; manual changes require no file ID. A current-balance materialization may be used for speed but is updated atomically with ledger state. All arithmetic is overflow-checked; preserve ledger history needed for idempotency and reconciliation.

Each accepted upload has one authoritative credit-entitlement record: original uploader, identity, accepted content size, captured earning policy, actual byte award frozen at first approval and approval state (`Pending | Approved | Revoked | Disabled`), plus a monotonic revision. State transitions are serialized, idempotent and reconcile to **one current contribution**: approving twice cannot credit twice; revoking twice cannot debit twice; reapproving adds it exactly once. Enforce this through unique transaction/transition IDs and transactional state checks, not UI behavior. Concurrent operator clicks, request retries, crashes and replays must be safe. Deleting an accepted file does not implicitly revoke its credit; toggling an entitlement never mutates unrelated manual adjustments.

Allow operator grants, deductions and atomic `Set balance` (including reset to zero from negative or positive) using ledger adjustments calculated from the locked authoritative current balance. Repeating a zero reset when already zero is a no-op. Negative balances are legal after credit revocation/deductions; they block additional credit-restricted downloads until replenished, but do not rewrite completed transfers. Manual `Set balance` sets the authoritative settled balance `B` atomically; outstanding reservations are **revocable**, not guaranteed entitlements. After any credit change, recompute active transfer budgets under the same account serialization and pause affected outgoing FileShare transfers promptly, even when revocation invalidates a previously reserved amount. Prioritize retained reservations deterministically by original admission order, never thread race order; if negative, pause all credit-restricted transfers. Optional reason fields and bounded history views support review. Pending approval is displayed separately and never spendable.

### 3.2 Byte-only credit arithmetic and transfer charges

Define `B` as the settled signed byte-credit balance, **including credits reserved for transfers**, `R = sum(R_i)` as total outstanding reservations, and `B - R` as spendable balance. Reserving credits increases `R` without changing `B`; releasing reservations decreases `R` without changing `B`. Settling newly confirmed chargeable bytes debits `B` and consumes the corresponding reservation atomically. Negative `B` remains permitted. For an existing transfer `i`, its budget includes its own retained reservation: `B - (R - R_i)`, not `B - R`; other transfers' reservations remain unavailable. Rebudget reservations under account serialization in original admission order after deductions/revocations, pausing transfers whose remaining requirement cannot be retained.

Define `credit_ratio = downloadable_bytes / accepted_uploaded_bytes`, as a bounded reduced positive rational `n/d` (separate explicit unrestricted/disabled states). Each account and immutable earning-policy revision has a durable remainder `r`, initially zero, with `0 <= r < d`. **Only first credit approval** advances it: compute checked `t = accepted_bytes * n + r`, whole potential award `q = floor(t/d)`, and new remainder `r = t mod d`. Atomically persist the remainder, actual award, entitlement transition and ledger effect. Approval order is the durable account serialization order; old pending uploads retain their captured policy revision and accumulator. A new policy revision starts a new accumulator rather than reinterpreting history. Zero-byte and zero-whole-byte awards still become immutable first-approved entitlements.

Apply earned-credit cap `C` to settled `B`, including reserved funds, never to spendable `B - R`: the first actual award is `min(q, max(0, C - B))`, or `q` when uncapped, using checked arithmetic. Discard clipped **whole credits permanently**, while retaining the fractional remainder even when the cap clips the entire whole award. Revocation subtracts only the frozen actual award and does **not** reverse fractional accumulation. Reapproval restores exactly that same award without advancing the accumulator, recalculating cap headroom, or recovering discarded whole credits. Subsequent first approvals under that policy may use the retained fraction. Initial/free credits and manual adjustments are explicit transactions; manual grants can explicitly bypass the earned-credit cap. Overflow/invalid ratios fail admission/configuration atomically.

When exchange is enabled and credit requirements apply, a file download uses a persistent `TransferId` and feature-owned `ChargeId`. Ordinary FileShare content downloads are chargeable; FileShare previews/thumbnails and catalog exports **in either direction**, and message/profile attachments and other unrelated feature transfers, are never chargeable and earn no upload credits. Trust a locally authorized immutable transfer-purpose classification, never remote MIME type, filename, extension or an arbitrary peer-supplied free flag. Quotas, authorization, generic transfer integrity and scheduling still apply to exempt transfers. The generic transport defined in `../protocol.md` has **no knowledge of credits or VFS**; the owning FileShare feature subscribes to durable checkpoint events and controls admission/pausing.

Before starting or resuming a chargeable download, calculate expected remaining byte cost from offered size and confirmed uniquely charged prefix. For new admission, default policy requires `B - R` to cover the remaining byte cost; resumption/rebudgeting of an existing transfer also counts its own retained reservation as defined above; an explicit per-contact policy may permit partial-credit transfer. Reserve anticipated remaining credit atomically across concurrent transfers without immediately debiting it. A reservation is revocable on credit policy/ledger changes, not protected from operator deductions or upload-entitlement revocation. At each acknowledged **durable mutually verified** sender/receiver prefix checkpoint, settle only new uniquely confirmed bytes for this transfer. Ordinary flow-control FileAck alone does not establish chargeable progress. Persist charge progress recoverably with the checkpoint; retries, resumed retransmissions and late duplicate acknowledgements never add charges. Cancelled/disconnected transfers retain settled charges; a new transfer to the same file starts a new charge identity, whereas resumption preserves the original. When settlement ordering after a crash is ambiguous, resolve uncertain charges in favor of the contact, never charge twice.

On admission or when credit falls below the remaining required budget, enter `WaitingForCredit` before transmission or persistent `PausedInsufficientCredit` during transmission, respectively; report machine-readable reason, shortfall, bytes confirmed and resume ability. No more payload windows are admitted after a deficit is established; in-flight bounded windows reconcile normally. Existing charged bytes remain charged. Resuming requires current READ/mount/assignment authorization, unchanged immutable transfer content, a mutually verified sender-chosen checkpoint, and fresh sufficient reservation (or explicitly permitted partial transfer). Transfer pause/resume and active-credit rebudgeting are serialized with account state, not guaranteed by stale cached balances. Credit recovery may trigger optional user-configured automatic resume; default to manual resume unless explicitly enabled.

The sender incrementally computes SHA-256 of the logical complete source bytes **as transmitted**, counting every logical byte exactly once, and persists confirmed prefix digests at durable checkpoints. On resume it recomputes the source prefix and verifies the saved digest before sending from the sender-selected agreed checkpoint; on completion it compares its complete digest with the originally offered digest. A sender-detected prefix or final mismatch terminates the transfer as `SourceIntegrityMismatch` and **refunds every settled byte debit tied to its ChargeId exactly once**, even across several resumed sessions. An incorrect receiver checkpoint digest fails/rejects the checkpoint as `ReceiverCheckpointMismatch`, without an automatic refund; it is not local proof that the source changed. Other unverified receiver claims of corruption likewise do not cause automatic refunds, but may be reviewed and compensated manually. A temporarily unavailable source pauses instead of being classified as corruption. A modification timestamp change alone pauses for revalidation and never proves a refundable source-integrity mismatch. Generic checkpoint backpressure and final confirmation follow `../protocol.md` for all transfers independently of exchange accounting. See `../protocol.md` for the generic checkpoint/resume wire contract.



### 3.3 Ledger bounds and recovery



Maintain authoritative compact account balance/checkpoint, persistent current per-upload entitlement state, active transfer charge progress/reservations and a bounded set of idempotency receipts. Detailed operator audit history is optional and may be disabled or time/size limited without affecting accounting. Append-only events may be compacted into atomic checkpoints, but **never** discard active entitlements or unsettled charges. Version the accounting epoch; reject replayed old IDs beyond a finite replay window rather than treating them as fresh work. Keep minimal durable terminal identifiers/tombstones for operations which can still reappear, within explicit expiry constraints. Bounded ledger growth is enforced with configurable entry/byte quotas, safe checkpoint/compaction and crash tests; if correctness-critical metadata cannot be retained/compacted, fail closed on **new chargeable work**, but retain operator emergency revocation capability. Partial recovery cannot produce an extra contact debit or double-earned credit. Ledger writes, checkpoint persistence and transfer progress across databases use the coordinator journal's idempotent recovery steps.

The upload coordinator in `fileshare_uploads.md` is an optional accounting participant when credits are enabled, not part of the basic upload success criterion. Earning policy is captured only for uploads accepted while the feature is enabled; no historical uploads are backfilled upon enabling.

## 4. Credit administration and tests

The identity settings switch is independently available even before credit administration is implemented, but must remain **off/disabled and non-actionable** until the ledger subsystem is ready. Once built, credit administration provides account balances, reservations, effective policy, first-approval entitlements, revoke/reapprove, manual grant/deduction/set balance, operator history and relevant paused transfers using the existing UI contract in `fileshare_ui.md`.

Focused tests for this later milestone: new/upgraded identity defaults off; off allows normal authorized FTP transfers and accepted uploads without credit traffic; enabling with no restrictive policy leaves downloads unrestricted; enabled credit requirement blocks and resumes appropriately; first approval and revocation/idempotency; toggle while active transfer reconciles unique verified prefix without double debit/credit; disable then re-enable preserves ledger history. After interactive acceptance, add comprehensive rationals, crash windows, reservation rebudgeting, negative balances, source-mismatch refunds and replay/fault matrices.

**No-goals:** generic transport modifications, accounting for conversation/media/catalog transfers, changing upload acceptance, or requiring credits for normal FTP use.
