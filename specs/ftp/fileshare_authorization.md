# DarkSpeak FileShare Authorization — basic access and resource policies

**Status:** Core FTP permission/authorization specification. Required for the first **interactive read-only browsing and download** milestone. **Separate later optional features:** `fileshare_uploads.md` (upload acceptance/quarantine/recovery) and `fileshare_credits.md` (optional byte exchange, **disabled by default per identity**). Neither upload coordination nor ledger availability is a prerequisite for ordinary LIST, STAT, catalog export or authorized downloads.

## 1. Scope and ownership

Implement identity-scoped FileShare authorization over effective per-contact VFS in `../vfs.md`. One production evaluator supplies remote server authorization decisions and administrator "View as Contact" with full provenance. Storage registry assignments, backend exclusions, filesystem capability/confinement and system resource protections are hard limits, never privileges. Policies and groups are persisted, versioned and updated atomically with authorization generations. Group membership belongs to the general identity-scoped contacts/groups subsystem; this module owns FileShare rules only. No independent filesystem or transfer engine.

Every remote request is bound to authenticated contact and local identity, selected effective mount, source namespace/object, requested operation, logical virtual path and policy/assignment generations. Paths and node IDs are not authority. Onion address is not user identity. Recheck admission and active operation/commit permissions, with revocation serialized at the irreversible boundary. Fail closed for missing credentials and invalid source bindings. No physical file paths or hidden objects escape through remote responses.

A user may share files for ordinary free downloads **without enabling credits**. The identity's `credits_enabled` switch is in `fileshare_credits.md` and defaults false; when false, no credit balance or ledger can deny an otherwise authorized download. Ordinary file transfers retain admission/space limits, integrity and authorization. Upload acceptance is a distinct operation and is specified in `fileshare_uploads.md`.

## 2. Permission rules and evaluation

At identity, group, and contact tiers, persist rules on virtual paths/mounts with independently tri-state permissions `UNSPECIFIED | ALLOW | DENY` for at least LIST, READ/DOWNLOAD, UPLOAD/CREATE, WRITE/REPLACE, RENAME, DELETE, RECURSE and EXPORT (extend through versioned operation definitions). A separate path-level `DENIED` hides/blocks a subtree. Identity defaults ultimately resolve every permission. Rules can modify permissions without defining a mount. The VFS composes mounts; this engine evaluates their effective access and retains mount provenance.

For **each permission**: resolve the most specific explicit applicable contact rule; otherwise resolve each applicable group independently by path specificity, then merge group results (`DENY` > `ALLOW` > `UNSPECIFIED`); otherwise take the most specific explicit identity rule or definitive identity default. `UNSPECIFIED` inherits. Higher tier wins over lower tier regardless of depth. Path `DENIED` is a separate subtree barrier, overridden only by an applicable explicit grant at a higher tier for the requested path; a reopened descendant has synthetic navigation-only ancestors, not access to their denied source contents. Evaluate relevant ancestor barriers as well as operation-specific decisions. A denial of READ need not deny LIST.

`RECURSE` permits implicit descent into ordinary source subdirectories within the selected mount; `RECURSE=DENY` does not hide direct files or independently authorized explicit child mounts. Virtual navigation to an explicitly mounted descendant is distinct from source recursion. Explicit path `DENIED` blocks child mounts until a higher-tier rule reopens the relevant virtual route. Parent mount replacement shadows inherited lower-tier descendants; permission-only rules do not change topology. Conflicting incompatible group source mounts at the same path remain inaccessible with a coalesced local administrator notification. Never infer permissions from a differently mounted occurrence of the same backend object.

EXPORT authorizes initiating a complete catalog export at its requested root; every emitted entry and traversed directory still requires normal LIST/RECURSE/path authorization. EXPORT never bypasses LIST, DENIED, mount limits, assignment limits or hidden metadata, and need not be explicitly allowed on each descendant. Ordinary LIST does not imply EXPORT. Preview and thumbnail requests must remain independently authorized and do not expose contents through a metadata privilege.

Define and regression-test exact precedence for inherited path rules, multi-group conflicts, reopened denied ancestors, synthetic mount containers, aliases, nested mounts and assignment boundaries. A resolved authorization result reports `Allowed | Denied | Hidden | TemporarilyUnavailable` with a safe machine-readable reason, policy generation and complete evaluated provenance; remote replies collapse hidden/denied existence as required by VFS privacy rules.

## 3. General quotas and policy inheritance

For ordinary FileShare resource policy, resolve each independent optional field contact → merged groups → identity, using the most specific explicit rule per field at a tier. `UNSPECIFIED` inherits; explicit `Unlimited` and `Disabled` are distinct from zero where appropriate. Group merging for **hard safety ceilings** (physical/staging/temporary/cache budgets, bandwidth and transfer concurrency) chooses the smallest finite limit. Upload acceptance/duplicate/quarantine rules live in `fileshare_uploads.md`; earning ratios, credit allowances and approval live in `fileshare_credits.md`. Limits enforced by FileManager/backend, registration and system policy always prevail over application grants. Quota changes affect future admissions and appropriate active-boundary rechecks; do not retroactively delete existing stored content.

## 4. Resource budgets and physical-space protection

Resource controls have two orthogonal dimensions: **scope** and **usage class**.

Scopes: shared physical storage pool/filesystem, backend, namespace, identity, contact, and active session/request. Classes: (1) permanent accepted content, (2) incomplete incoming transfer/staging, (3) operation-temporary artifacts such as complete VFS catalog exports and generated directory listings, (4) disposable reusable caches such as thumbnails and immutable catalog exports. All classes count toward common physical capacity while retaining independent class budgets for bytes, objects, outstanding reservations, concurrent writers/generators, leases and retention. Temporary artifacts and caches are private storage objects, never normal VFS files merely because they exist.

System/backend guards include configurable minimum **available bytes**, minimum **available percent** and maximum backend/pool-used bytes (e.g. stop new admissions below 10% free or above 123 GiB backend usage), with optional more restrictive critical low-space handling for active uploads/generation. Both percent and byte minimums must hold. Check pool capacity including all active reservations, retained old generations, recovery needs and other DarkSpeak namespaces sharing the physical pool. FileManager/backend enforces durable capacity reservations, quota accounting, lifecycle, cleanup and platform space checks; the host coordinates shared-pool hard ceilings where multiple backends share a filesystem. These are absolute, not mergeable user privileges. Re-evaluate admission and growth, pause/reject safely on threshold changes; cleanup/recovery must retain headroom. Another local process may fill the disk, so handle actual `DiskFull` independently.

Use independent, bounded temporary-artifact creation and read leases via an **optional common `IFileManager` capability** with default `UnsupportedCapability`, not implementation casts in normal VFS/server code. Finalize and hash a catalog artifact before transmission; bind a lease to the authorized VFS request, revoke future reads on permission withdrawal, and bound resume retention. Cache items are evictable when unleased; promote finalized temporary objects to immutable cache objects without payload duplication where supported, but that optimization is optional. Per-contact and per-identity temporary admission caps protect against catalog-export spam; equivalent work sharing is optional later. Exhausting temporary/cache budgets must not prevent routine permanent-file reads.

Application policy decides **who may request** temporary work and admission budgets. FileManager enforces **what is actually reserved, stored and reclaimed**. The VFS produces authorized catalog data but does not implement temporary physical storage or quotas. Expose safe generic resource-unavailable reasons remotely; keep actual capacity figures and internal paths local.

## 5. Decisions, revocation and inspection UI

One effective-policy resolver produces decision and full structured explanation for actual requests and the administrator's "View as Contact" inspection. Preserve every evaluated contributing rule (including overridden group ALLOW/DENY and inherited decisions) with tier, path, group/contact/rule stable IDs, provenance, effective value and whether it was decisive. The UI colors individual permission contributions by tier and uses each group's own icon/color, including losing contributors; effective result remains obvious with accessible text/icons, not color alone. Present effective resource quotas and limiting safety ceilings and the reason for unavailable operations.

Configuration, group membership, storage-assignment, policy and mount changes advance generations, invalidate derived views, authorization-bound cursors/subscriptions and cached permissions, and revoke affected readers, prepared/snapshot leases, writers and pending commits at safe boundaries. Recheck during subsequent reads and before irreversible commit. Authorization withdrawal is never delayed merely because the storage backend already prepared a file. Restrict operator administration, resource/permission administration to privileged local context. Publish changes and decision provenance without leaking denied names, hidden counts, physical paths or identity-private data to remote contacts.

## 6. Initial implementation and tests

**First usable FTP milestone:** persist identity/group/contact permission and resource rules; resolve effective per-contact VFS and provenance; enforce LIST/STAT/nonrecursive browse, optional EXPORT/RECURSE, prepared READ/download and active revocation through `fileshare_server.md` and `fileshare_protocol.md`. No upload acceptance workflow or credit accounting is required until their later milestones.

During implementation test basic contact allow/deny, group conflict, assigned-subtree limits, blocked hidden entries, LIST versus READ/EXPORT distinction, permission changes while a request is pending, restart persistence and safe revocation. Expand full group/path precedence, alias, recovery and malicious-input matrices after two-instance read-only FTP is interactively exercised, without delaying security-critical checks.

**No-goals:** upload workflow implementation, credit ledger, user-visible transfer scheduler, or native physical-name exposure.
